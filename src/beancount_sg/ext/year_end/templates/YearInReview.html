How accounts are classified

Each asset and liability account's cash-flow-role and net worth group, and the income and expense accounts counted as investment returns or transfers. Declare a role on the account's open directive, where it also applies to subaccounts; without one, assets are cash if their name contains Cash, Checking, Savings or Bank, else investing, liabilities are financing, and income and expense accounts operating, except debt-payment accounts (e.g. Expenses:CreditCardPayment), which are financing.

2020-01-01 open Assets:Bank:CD
  cash-flow-role: "investing"

2020-01-01 open Assets:Car
  investable: FALSE

Assets with the cash role are cash, and other assets are investments, unless marked investable: FALSE (e.g. a home or a car): property. Liabilities with the financing role, the default, are debt. Income and expense accounts declared with a role other than operating are left out of income, expenses and savings: investing ones, e.g. capital gains, are investment returns, and others, e.g. a loan-repayment expense account declared financing, transfers.

2020-01-01 open Income:CapitalGains
  cash-flow-role: "investing"

2020-01-01 open Expenses:LoanRepayment
  cash-flow-role: "financing"

How to use
  • The review shows the years of Fava's time filter, or up to five years ending with its year (or this year, without one), at most five. Fava's account and advanced filters apply, but balances always include everything before the years shown.
  • Values are in the first operating currency, rounded to whole units: holdings at market value at each year's end (or at cost without a price), income and expenses at their value on each transaction's date.
  • Balances are at each year's end (this year: today), and income, expenses, savings and debt paid down are during the year (this year: so far). The spending ratios use the 12 months of spending up to the year's end, against balances then: for this year, the last 12 months, so they compare with whole years.
  • Changes from the previous year are green when they're good (e.g. more savings, less spending or debt) and red when they're bad. A year-to-date year's income and spending aren't compared with whole years.
  • The withdrawal rate is spending as a share of investable assets (cash and investments), like a withdrawal rate in retirement; spending / net worth also counts property and debt.
  • Click a year for its income statement, a category or loan for its account page, or a bar's date in a chart to make that the final year. Below the net worth chart, switch between net worth and assets & liabilities.
