How accounts are classified

Each account's cash-flow-role: cash accounts are the cash the statement explains, and every other account's flows are a line in its role's section. Declare a role on the account's open directive, where it also applies to subaccounts:

2020-01-01 open Assets:MoneyMarket
  cash-flow-role: "cash"

Without one, assets named with Cash, Checking, Savings or Bank are cash, other assets investing, liabilities and equity financing, and income and expenses operating: the same convention as Beancount.io's cash-flow report.

How to use
  • The period is Fava's time filter, up to today, or the last 12 months (this month and the 11 before) without one. Fava's account and advanced filters apply, as on Fava's own reports.
  • The cards name each activity's total in plain terms: net income (operating), invested or withdrawn from investments (investing), and debt paid down or borrowed (financing). Only net income is green (a surplus) or red (a deficit): investing and financing aren't good or bad by which way they go.
  • The statement and charts read the same way as the cards: income is positive and spending negative, money put into investments positive, and debt payments positive (new borrowing negative). The change in cash is net income, minus invested, minus debt paid down. Income and expenses are rolled up to their top-level category, e.g. Expenses:Food.
  • The diagram shows the same totals: what brought money into cash on the left (income, and money taken out of investments or borrowed) and where it went on the right (expenses, investing, paying down debt), with the net cash saved (or drawn) balancing them, through the period's total cash flow in the middle. Click income or expenses for the income statement, investing or debt for the balance sheet.
  • The emergency fund is cash at the end, in months of average spending over the 12 months up to it, whatever the period.
  • Credit cards are financing, like any liability: spending on a card is in operating, by category, and the card's line in financing is its net change. A card paid in full nets to about zero; one carrying a balance shows as borrowing or repaying it.
  • Values are in the first operating currency, rounded to whole units: flows at each transaction's date, cash at market value at the start and end. A change in value line shows any difference, e.g. from exchange rates.
  • Click a line or cash account for its account page. Below the chart, switch between net income, invested, debt paid down and the change in cash; click a bar's date for that interval.
